# IC Memo — An Illustrative GLP-1 Asset Entering Obesity

**Sample deliverable.** The asset below is illustrative and does not represent a real company. Every *clinical comparator* claim is sourced to the public record; any *modelled* figure is flagged as illustrative. This sample exists to show the structure and the sourcing discipline of a real engagement, not to make a recommendation.

---

## Recommendation (illustrative)

Conditional interest. The obesity market rewards magnitude of weight loss, tolerability at the maintenance dose, and a credible oral or low-burden route. An asset that cannot credibly clear the tirzepatide efficacy bar, or differentiate on tolerability or route, is underwriting a commodity entry into a market with two entrenched, outcomes-backed injectable incumbents — and now a credible oral one. Orforglipron (Foundayo), FDA-approved in April 2026, has shown an oral small-molecule GLP-1 can win on convenience and in the maintenance setting, but it has not yet matched injectable depth of weight loss — and it is not taking the injectables' share on magnitude. The opening for a new oral is therefore real but narrow: it has to beat the established oral on depth or durability, not merely match its route. Next-generation orals still in development keep that lane contestable.

## The asset

"Asset X" — an illustrative oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist, positioned for 1L obesity (BMI ≥ 30, or ≥ 27 with a comorbidity). Phase 2 readout assumed pending. The questions that decide the check are below.

## The competitive bar (sourced)

The bar a new 1L obesity asset must clear is set by two incumbents:

| Comparator | Mean weight loss | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide 2.4 mg (s.c. weekly) | ~14.9% at 68 wks | STEP-1, NEJM 2021 | PMID 33567185 |
| Tirzepatide 15 mg (s.c. weekly) | ~20.9% at 72 wks | SURMOUNT-1, NEJM 2022 | PMID 35658024 |
| Tirzepatide vs semaglutide (head-to-head) | 20.2% vs 13.7% at 72 wks | SURMOUNT-5, NEJM 2025 | NCT05822830 |
| Orforglipron / Foundayo 36 mg (oral, once-daily) | ~12.4% at 72 wks | ATTAIN-1, NEJM 2025; FDA-approved Apr 2026 | NCT05869903 |

The head-to-head SURMOUNT-5 (2025) settled the cross-trial guesswork: tirzepatide beat semaglutide directly on weight loss and on GI discontinuation. And the oral wedge is no longer theoretical — orforglipron (Foundayo, FDA-approved April 2026), an oral small-molecule GLP-1, posted ~12.4% in Phase 3 (ATTAIN-1, 2025). Orals are now winning on convenience and in the maintenance setting, but they have not yet matched the ~20% depth of weight loss from the best injectables, and are not taking the injectables' share on magnitude. A new oral entrant is therefore measured against a real, approved oral incumbent — with genuine room to compete, but on depth or durability rather than route alone. Next-generation orals still in development keep that lane contestable.

Semaglutide has since added a cardiovascular-outcomes claim (SELECT, NCT03574597; Lincoff et al., NEJM 2023), raising the bar from weight loss to demonstrated CV benefit in patients with established disease. Any new entrant is now competing not just on the scale but on the outcomes story.

## What has to be true

1. **Efficacy.** Phase 2 weight loss credibly trending toward the tirzepatide range, or a clear tolerability/route advantage that justifies a lower number.
2. **Tolerability at the maintenance dose.** GI discontinuation is the category's real ceiling. The differentiator is not peak loss; it is the dose patients can stay on.
3. **Route / burden.** A genuine oral with competitive exposure is a real wedge against weekly injectables — but the wedge is now contested. Orforglipron (Foundayo, FDA-approved April 2026; ATTAIN-1, 2025) showed an oral small-molecule GLP-1 can deliver ~12% in Phase 3: orals are winning on convenience and in maintenance, but have not yet matched the ~20% depth of the best injectables and are not yet taking their share on magnitude. So a new oral must beat the established oral on depth or durability, not just match its route — a me-too oral that only matches convenience is not a wedge, and a me-too injectable is no wedge at all.
4. **Durability and the regain question.** The market is moving toward maintenance and CV outcomes, not 72-week loss alone.

## Risks

- **Incumbent moat.** Two outcomes-backed products with manufacturing scale and payer relationships. Differentiation has to be real, not narrative.
- **Tolerability tail.** Class GI effects can cap the effective dose below the headline.
- **Reimbursement.** Coverage is tightening to outcomes; a weight-loss-only label is a weaker commercial position than it was two years ago.
- **Modelled economics (illustrative).** Any rNPV or peak-sales figure in a full memo would be built from explicit, stated assumptions and labelled as modelled — not asserted. None is claimed here.

## What would change the view

A Phase 2 readout clearing ~15% mean weight loss with a discontinuation rate at or below the incumbents, on an oral route. That combination would move this from conditional interest to active.
