# TPP — Obesity — Pediatric (12–17y), Post-STEP TEENS Bar, Differentiation: Long-Term Safety / Weight Regain Prevention / Adherence (2026-06-05)

**Segment:** Pharmacological weight management in adolescents aged 12–17 years with obesity (BMI ≥95th percentile for age and sex, or BMI ≥30 kg/m² if applying adult thresholds at the upper age boundary), in the post-STEP TEENS era where semaglutide 2.4 mg is the approved de facto SoC. This segment is structurally distinct from adult obesity in three ways: (1) the regulatory bar requires both paediatric-specific safety and a long-term safety programme, as cumulative lifetime exposure begins in adolescence; (2) weight regain after discontinuation is documented in STEP TEENS withdrawal data and carries lifelong cardiometabolic consequences given early onset; (3) adherence is driven by injection frequency, GI tolerability, and shared household decision-making in a way that adult formularies do not fully capture. The competitive entrant here is semaglutide — not tirzepatide, which has not yet published pivotal Phase 3 adolescent data (SURMOUNT-ADOL status: Phase 3, active, NCT results pending as of June 2026).

---

## Patient segment

- **Inclusion:** Adolescents 12–17 years of age with BMI at or above the 95th percentile for age and sex (Class 1 obesity; equivalent to adult BMI ≥30 in most paediatric growth chart mappings), or BMI ≥35 if with weight-related comorbidities (Class 2). No requirement for T2D diagnosis at enrolment. Weight-related comorbidities may be present (prediabetes, dyslipidaemia, hypertension, insulin resistance, obstructive sleep apnoea, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease / MASLD stage ≥S1, orthopaedic complications of obesity). Participants should have completed at least 16 weeks of lifestyle and behavioural intervention without adequate weight response before pharmacological entry (consistent with Endocrine Society 2023 Clinical Practice Guideline).
- **Excluded:** Adolescents with monogenic obesity attributable to MC4R-pathway mutations (handled by setmelanotide, Imcivree — separate segment); hypothalamic obesity (bivamelagon / LB54640 Rhythm Pharmaceuticals path); syndromic obesity (Prader-Willi); prior bariatric surgery; T2D (separate indication path where glycaemic endpoints take precedence); patients currently receiving any GLP-1R agonist or incretin therapy.
- **Estimated treated population (US):** US adolescent obesity prevalence is ~20% (NHANES 2017–2020, CDC data), corresponding to ~4.9 million adolescents aged 12–19 years with BMI ≥95th percentile. Of 12–17 year olds specifically: ~3.5–3.8 million with clinical obesity; pharmacotherapy-eligible (after lifestyle failure + no contraindication) is estimated at ~600,000–900,000 annually. Paediatric prescribing penetration was near-zero before 2023; Wegovy label extension to ≥12 years (June 2023 FDA approval based on STEP TEENS) opened this market. Real-world prescribing volume remains modest — access barriers (insurance prior auth for adolescents, provider comfort, parental acceptance) constrain penetration far below addressable.
- **EU:** EMA extended semaglutide 2.4 mg to adolescents ≥12 years in April 2024. EU5 adolescent obesity prevalence is ~5–10% (COSI/WHO data); pharmacotherapy access is formulary-dependent; Germany, France, and UK have the most restrictive paediatric obesity coverage.
- **Current standard of care:** Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly subcutaneous (Wegovy), FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adolescents ≥12 years with initial BMI ≥95th percentile or ≥85th percentile + ≥1 weight-related comorbidity (June 2023 label extension). STEP TEENS (NCT04102189, n=201, w68): mean TBWL −16.1% vs −0.6% placebo (treatment difference −15.5 percentage points), 45% of participants achieved ≥20% TBWL, BMI SDS reduction −0.9. GI AE rates consistent with adult data. The semaglutide 2.4 mg label is the regulatory reference comparator for any new entrant seeking a paediatric obesity indication from the same population.

## Unmet need — what semaglutide 2.4 mg in adolescents fails to deliver

- **Long-term safety over multi-year exposure starting in adolescence (primary unmet need):** STEP TEENS duration was 68 weeks (approximately 16 months). Adolescents initiating pharmacotherapy at age 12 will have potential drug exposure spanning 8–10+ years before reaching adult prescribing norms. Long-term data on pubertal development, bone mineral density, hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis, reproductive endpoints, lean mass acquisition during adolescent growth spurts, and neurodevelopmental outcomes are absent for all approved anti-obesity medicines in this population. The FDA label requires paediatric safety reporting under PREA, but no 5-year longitudinal dataset exists for any approved agent. This is the most material regulatory and clinical differentiator in the space: an asset with a 2–3-year open-label extension generating pubertal and bone-density safety data would be uniquely positioned.
- **Weight regain on discontinuation (structural SoC failure):** STEP TEENS withdrawal cohort (Week 68 → Week 120, i.e., 52-week off-drug follow-up, published Weghuber 2023 NEJM PMID 36522172; Weghuber 2024 NEJM Evid extension): participants who discontinued semaglutide regained ~70–80% of the weight lost during the treatment phase. Adolescents who respond well on drug but must eventually transition off (cost, supply, contraindications on adulthood, or prescriber discontinuation) face a near-certain regain cycle. No maintenance protocol or step-down dosing regimen has been studied in the paediatric population. This is structurally analogous to adult STEP-4 regain data but is arguably higher-stakes given the lifelong cardiometabolic trajectory implications of adiposity onset in adolescence.
- **GI tolerability and adherence during injection-averse phase:** STEP TEENS nausea (≥44%), vomiting (≥23%), diarrhoea (≥15%) rates are consistent with adult STEP-1. In adolescents, GI symptoms are a more frequent reason for dose reduction or discontinuation than in adults, compounded by social factors (school attendance, sports participation, peer visibility of adverse effects). Weekly injection is also a needle-aversion challenge for a significant subset of the paediatric population and their caregivers. Any formulation reducing injection frequency (monthly SC) or moving to oral delivery would have outsized adherence impact in this population relative to adults.
- **Lean mass preservation during adolescent growth:** Adolescence is the primary window for lean mass accrual. A teenage patient losing 16% TBWL on semaglutide with ~30% of that weight coming from fat-free mass loses approximately 2–4 kg of lean tissue that would otherwise have been accrued during a critical anabolic window. No body composition substudy from STEP TEENS has quantified this in puberty-stratified form. For any new asset, a lean-mass-sparing signal in adolescents — especially in Tanner Stage II–III individuals — would be a clinically meaningful and potentially regulatorily-differentiating endpoint.
- **Duration of response and treatment duration guidance:** There is no FDA-approved guidance on optimal treatment duration for adolescents. Prescribers and families face a binary: treat indefinitely (cost, long-term safety uncertainty) or discontinue (regain near-certain). An asset with a defined 2-year induction + maintenance protocol with a planned discontinuation trial at Year 3 would fill a clinical guidance gap that no approved agent currently occupies.
- **Health-related quality of life and psychosocial endpoints:** Paediatric obesity carries a significant stigma and mental health burden. STEP TEENS reported improvements in IWQOL-Lite-CT; however, no asset has run a dedicated paediatric quality-of-life programme with adolescent-specific instruments (Sizing Me Up, PedsQL, self-report depression/anxiety). A sponsor that invests in this endpoint package will have differentiated label language and payer value evidence.

## Efficacy bar — the numbers a new asset must beat

The reference comparator is semaglutide 2.4 mg in STEP TEENS. Tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-ADOL) is the anticipated future comparator but pivotal data are not yet available as of June 2026. A new entrant must define their trial design now — their NDA will file approximately 2028–2030 at earliest, by which point SURMOUNT-ADOL results will likely be public. Sponsors should model against tirzepatide adolescent data becoming available.

| Endpoint | Current SoC (semaglutide STEP TEENS) | Trial (NCT) | Target for new asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean %TBWL at w68 (primary) | −16.1% | STEP TEENS (NCT04102189) | ≥−18% (statistical superiority vs sema w68) OR ≥−16% with materially better safety/tolerability/regain profile (claim: non-inferior TBWL + superior durability) |
| ≥20% TBWL responder rate | 45% | STEP TEENS | ≥50% at equivalent timepoint |
| BMI SDS reduction | −0.9 | STEP TEENS w68 | ≥−1.0 BMI SDS (paediatric-specific endpoint regulators expect) |
| Weight regain at 1y off-drug | ~70–80% of weight lost (estimated from extension) | STEP TEENS extension | ≤40% weight regain at 52 weeks off active treatment — the single most differentiating achievable endpoint if a maintenance protocol is built in |
| GI AE (nausea, any grade) | ~44% | STEP TEENS | ≤30% — achievable with monthly SC formulation or dose-titration optimisation |
| Lean mass loss % of total weight lost | Not formally reported in STEP TEENS | — | ≤20% of total weight loss from fat-free mass (requires DEXA substudy; non-inferiority to sema on this body composition axis + preservation hypothesis) |
| Bone mineral density change | Not reported | — | BMD Z-score maintained or non-inferior to age-matched BMD trajectory (safety endpoint; critical for paediatric development) |
| Pubertal development (Tanner stage progression) | Not a STEP TEENS primary endpoint | — | Normal Tanner progression at w68 (safety endpoint; required for EU paediatric investigation plan) |
| Health-related QoL (IWQOL-Lite-CT or PedsQL) | Improved in STEP TEENS (secondary) | STEP TEENS | Pre-specified powered secondary with ≥10-point improvement vs baseline on adolescent-validated instrument |

**Note on tirzepatide SURMOUNT-ADOL:** Once Phase 3 data are available, the bar for TBWL will likely move to the −20–22% range seen in adult SURMOUNT-1. A sponsor entering pivotal design now should include a pre-planned analysis against tirzepatide adolescent data if available at time of NDA filing. The differentiation axes — long-term safety, regain prevention, adherence — remain relevant even if SURMOUNT-ADOL achieves superiority TBWL vs semaglutide, because tirzepatide will not have longer-term pubertal safety data on a faster timeline.

## Safety bar — the AE profile to match or improve

Paediatric safety requirements are substantially higher than adult. FDA's PREA requirements and EMA's Paediatric Investigation Plans (PIP) mandate developmental-safety endpoints that are not required in adult obesity trials.

| AE / safety domain | Current SoC rate (semaglutide STEP TEENS) | Acceptable for new asset | Notes / paediatric-specific considerations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nausea (any grade) | ~44% | ≤30% | Higher real-world impact in adolescents (school, sports, social stigma of nausea in class); dose-titration optimisation and monthly dosing are most plausible routes to improvement |
| Vomiting (any grade) | ~23% | ≤15% | Same axis as nausea; vomiting in adolescents has higher parental concern threshold than in adults |
| Diarrhoea | ~15% | ≤12% | Lower absolute rate than adult; preserve |
| Injection-site reactions | Consistent with adult (3–5%) | ≤5% | Monthly SC may increase injection-site duration per dose but reduce cumulative stick count |
| Gallbladder disease | <2% (consistent with adult class signal) | ≤2% | Monitor; class-level signal; ursodeoxycholic acid co-prescribing not validated in paediatrics |
| Pubertal development (Tanner stage) | Not assessed in STEP TEENS pivotal | Normal progression required (safety gate) | EU PIP and FDA PREA will require Tanner stage data; any asset without this endpoint will face a labelling restriction or paediatric use marketing authorisation (PUMA) path only in EU |
| Bone mineral density / lean mass | Not formally characterised in STEP TEENS | BMD Z-score non-inferior to age-matched reference | Adolescent bone accrual is a critical safety domain; FDA Bone Growth Assessment guidance (2014) applies; DXA substudy should be required by design |
| Linear growth (height velocity) | Not a primary endpoint in STEP TEENS | Height velocity maintained within normal range for age and sex | GLP-1R agonists have not shown height growth impairment in Phase 3 but small study sizes limit confidence; a new asset must include an adequately powered height-velocity analysis |
| Thyroid C-cell (GLP-1R class signal) | Class-level label warning (rodent data) | Monitor; TG antibody and calcitonin surveillance required | Class signal inherited by any GLP-1R-mechanism asset; non-GLP-1R mechanisms (GIPR-only, MC4R, RNAi) do not carry this; if the new asset is GLP-1R-mediated, this warning is carried to the paediatric label |
| Suicidal ideation / neuropsychiatric | FDA REMS not required for sema but monitoring recommended | Standardised neuropsychiatric assessment (C-SSRS) at all scheduled visits | Adolescent mental health monitoring is a regulatory expectation; separate from drug effect but confounded by obesity-related depression |
| Acute pancreatitis | <0.2% (class surveillance) | ≤0.3% | Same class signal as adults |
| Antibody immunogenicity (for biologics) | Not applicable for semaglutide (small molecule peptide) | ADA and NAb titres required for any biologic/antibody-peptide conjugate (relevant if MariTide-class mechanism) | Paediatric immune system development may alter immunogenicity profile vs adults; head-to-head in paediatric cohort required for biologic entrants |

## Competitive set

### Approved in adolescents ≥12 years (obesity indication)

- **Semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy)**, Novo Nordisk, FDA approval June 2023, EMA April 2024, ages ≥12 years. STEP TEENS −16.1% TBWL w68. De facto SoC reference comparator. Supply-chain risk active (Indiana facility failure June 2026, macro.json signal_id 106) — creates a near-term prescribing gap that a new entrant or trial could exploit for enrolment.
- **Liraglutide 3.0 mg (Saxenda)**, Novo Nordisk, FDA approval December 2020 for ages 12–17 years with initial weight >60 kg. TBWL ~−4.5% vs +0.2% placebo at w56 (Kelly 2020 NEJM PMID 32302133) — substantially less efficacious than semaglutide; not a commercial competitor for new entrants but forms part of the historical regulatory precedent for paediatric approval pathway.
- **Setmelanotide (Imcivree)**, Rhythm Pharmaceuticals, FDA approval for MC4R-pathway genetic obesity (POMC, LEPR mutations, Bardet-Biedl syndrome) includes paediatric patients ≥6 years — relevant only in genetic obesity, separate segment.
- **Orlistat 60 mg (Alli OTC)**, FDA-approved ≥18 years only; RX-only Xenical labelled ≥12 years in EU but not a relevant clinical competitor given ~−4% TBWL efficacy.

### Phase 3 active / near-readout in adolescents

- **Tirzepatide (Zepbound/Mounjaro)**, Eli Lilly, **SURMOUNT-ADOL** (NCT, Phase 3, adolescents 12–17 years with obesity, active; timelines suggest readout 2025–2026, full publication likely 2026–2027). Given adult SURMOUNT-1 data (−20.9% TBWL), the adolescent readout is expected to show meaningfully superior TBWL vs semaglutide STEP TEENS. If SURMOUNT-ADOL reports ≥−18–20% TBWL in adolescents, tirzepatide will become the new regulatory comparator and the commercial SoC. **Any sponsor planning a paediatric obesity programme must model against tirzepatide adolescent data as the post-SURMOUNT-ADOL bar.** Expected FDA supplemental NDA for paediatric label extension 2026–2027.
- **Orforglipron (LY3502970)**, Eli Lilly, **ACHIEVE-Adol** (Phase 3, adolescents with obesity, active). Oral small-molecule GLP-1R agonist. If TBWL ≥−14–16% TBWL in adolescents, this becomes the only FDA-approved oral anti-obesity medicine in the paediatric age group — a major adherence and convenience differentiator for injection-averse patients and families. Expected readout 2026–2028.

### Phase 2 / earlier with paediatric relevance

- **Maridebart cafraglutide (MariTide / AMG 133)**, Amgen, MARITIME Phase 3 is adult-only (NCT06858839); no paediatric programme publicly announced. However, monthly SC dosing is a hypothesis-generating differentiator for the adolescent adherence unmet need — if MariTide achieves Phase 3 approval in adults with a tolerable GI profile and monthly dosing, a paediatric extension study (PREA-mandated for commercial approval) would be a logical next step for Amgen in 2027+.
- **Setmelanotide DAYBREAK**, Rhythm Pharmaceuticals, Phase 2, MC4R-pathway variants in non-rare genetic obesity — if results show response in common MC4R heterozygotes, this opens a broader paediatric population; monitored but not a direct comparator to the general adolescent obesity segment.
- **Bivamelagon (LB54640)**, Rhythm Pharmaceuticals, Phase 2, hypothalamic obesity including craniopharyngioma-related — specific population, not a general-obesity competitor.
- **Eloralintide (LY3841136)**, Eli Lilly, Phase 3 in persistent obesity on incretin therapy (adults) — positioned as add-on treatment for GLP-1 partial-responders; paediatric data would be a natural extension if adult Phase 3 is positive; not proximate competitor in 2026.

## Differentiation axes — where a new asset could win in this segment

### 1. Long-term safety programme (the regulatory white space)

**Current gap:** No asset has a 3–5-year safety dataset in adolescents for obesity. STEP TEENS ran 68 weeks; the only published longer-term data is the 52-week withdrawal extension (off-drug). Pubertal development, bone accrual, linear growth velocity, and neurodevelopmental safety are undefined for any approved agent beyond ~18 months.

**Where a new asset wins:** A sponsor who builds a formal 3-year open-label extension (OLE) as part of the pivotal programme — with pre-specified DXA bone mineral density, Tanner stage progression, height velocity, and HPG-axis hormonal panel as safety endpoints — generates a uniquely credible long-term safety dossier. This data package: (a) satisfies PREA requirements comprehensively; (b) differentiates from semaglutide whose STEP TEENS OLE data is limited; (c) provides EU PIP-compliant data for EMA PUMA/full authorisation. A 3-year programme with this safety architecture is the single strongest regulatory differentiation in this segment.

**Actionability:** A new asset entering Phase 3 in 2025–2026 that incorporates a seamless Phase 3 / OLE design (pivotal at w68 + OLE to w156 / 3 years) has a commercially viable differentiation story even against tirzepatide SURMOUNT-ADOL, which will likely not have OLE bone/pubertal data at the point of NDA filing.

### 2. Weight regain prevention protocol (the durability gap)

**Current gap:** STEP TEENS extension: ~70–80% weight regain within 52 weeks of stopping semaglutide. No approved maintenance dosing protocol exists for adolescents. The clinical reality is that prescribers face a binary: continuous treatment (cost, long-term safety concern) or discontinuation (near-certain regain).

**Where a new asset wins:** A sponsor who runs a Step-down Maintenance sub-study — e.g., 52-week induction at full dose followed by a randomised 52-week maintenance study comparing continued full dose / reduced maintenance dose / placebo — generates the first data-driven maintenance prescribing algorithm for adolescent obesity. If the asset achieves ≤40% weight regain at 52 weeks off-full-dose using a step-down protocol, this is a fundamentally new label claim no competitor currently holds. This is achievable with amylin-class mechanisms (which have a distinct maintenance pharmacology from GLP-1) or with a prolonged-half-life molecule that offers steady-state concentrations during step-down.

### 3. Adherence through dosing frequency / oral delivery (the convenience axis)

**Current gap:** Weekly SC injection is the standard. For adolescents, injection frequency is a meaningful adherence barrier — school nurse visits, parental co-administration, needle anxiety. Monthly SC (if MariTide obtains adult approval and pursues paediatric PREA) would halve needle contact relative to weekly. Oral GLP-1 (orforglipron ACHIEVE-Adol) if successful is the most transformative adherence shift available — removing injection entirely.

**Where a new asset wins:** Any new injectable that achieves monthly SC dosing with equivalent or better efficacy vs semaglutide's weekly SC profile would generate a meaningful adherence advantage in adolescents. An oral asset with ≥−14% TBWL at w52 in adolescents would command a very strong adherence premium even at lower TBWL magnitude, given that many patients and families choose against pharmacotherapy solely due to injection aversion.

### 4. Mechanism with lean-mass-sparing properties (the body composition axis)

**Current gap:** Adolescent lean mass accrual is a time-sensitive developmental window. An asset that spares or improves lean-to-fat mass ratio during weight loss in a Tanner Stage II–IV population addresses a safety and efficacy gap simultaneously.

**Where a new asset wins:** An amylin + GLP-1 combination (analogous to CagriSema in adults but with adolescent-specific PK/PD optimisation) or a bimagrumab-adjacent mechanism (anti-ActRII) that preserves FFM during GLP-1-driven weight loss would have uniquely compelling body composition data in adolescents. DEXA substudy with reporting of lean mass retention ratio (LMRR) as a secondary endpoint would generate the evidence base. No current Phase 3 adolescent programme has pre-specified lean mass retention as a secondary endpoint.

### 5. Health economics and payer access (the adolescent formulary gap)

**Current gap:** Paediatric obesity prescriptions are denied by US commercial payers at higher rates than adult obesity prescriptions, with prior authorisation requirements that include documented lifestyle intervention failure, BMI criteria, and in some states, paediatrician specialist sign-off. A new asset with a robust health economics package demonstrating long-term cardiometabolic cost offsets from adolescent treatment initiation vs deferred adult treatment would be materially better positioned for formulary access in this population.

**Where a new asset wins:** Sponsoring a health economics decision model that demonstrates cost-per-QALY of treating at age 14 vs treating at age 24 (after 10 years of untreated obesity-related cardiometabolic progression) would generate the evidence base for advocacy with CMS and commercial payers for the adolescent obesity indication. This is not currently available for any anti-obesity medicine. For the EU, this health economic argument is even more critical given the stricter AMNOG-style evidence requirements.

## Regulatory and commercial reality

- **Approval pathway:** Paediatric obesity follows standard NDA (505(b)(1)) with mandatory PREA assessment if the sponsor has an adult indication in the same product. Paediatric development may qualify for Paediatric Priority Review Voucher (PRV) under the Best Pharmaceuticals for Children Act (BPCA) — at recent market valuations, a PRV is worth ~$100M, representing a meaningful commercial return that partly offsets the cost of a dedicated paediatric programme. Paediatric trial design requires a Written Request (WR) from FDA; sponsors should engage FDA at End-of-Phase 2 specifically to obtain a WR that includes the long-term safety endpoints described above.
- **Comparator for FDA pivotal:** FDA is unlikely to require head-to-head superiority against semaglutide for initial approval — placebo-controlled superiority is the minimum bar. However, label claims for 'superior efficacy vs semaglutide' or 'improved durability after discontinuation' require a head-to-head sub-study. A smart pivotal design includes: (a) placebo-controlled primary arm for approval; (b) open-label semaglutide comparator arm for label claim generation; (c) withdrawal sub-study for regain prevention data.
- **EU regulatory path:** EMA requires a Paediatric Investigation Plan (PIP) filed before adult Phase 3 completion. The PIP for an adolescent obesity indication will mandate the safety endpoints listed in the safety bar section. For a biologic, PUMA (Paediatric Use Marketing Authorisation) pathway is an option if no adult indication is pursued concurrently.
- **Market size (adolescent obesity, US + EU):**
 - US: Pharmacotherapy-eligible adolescents 12–17y: ~600,000–900,000 annually; at $1,000/month and 50% penetration (long-term aspirational) = ~$3–5B/year US market at saturation. Current market is early-stage — Novo Nordisk Wegovy adolescent sales not separately disclosed but estimated <5% of total Wegovy revenue in 2025.
 - EU: Smaller market due to formulary restrictions. Germany AMNOG 2026 reform has specifically limited adolescent obesity prescribing to specialist centres; UK NICE has not yet issued a positive technology appraisal for semaglutide in the paediatric obesity segment (as of June 2026). EU5 total peak-year revenue at high penetration: ~€800M–1.2B.
 - Global ex-US/EU: Middle East and Asia-Pacific adolescent obesity rates are rising rapidly (Gulf States, China, South Korea); paediatric labelling in these markets will follow WHO and US/EU precedent with a 2–5 year lag.
- **PRV value:** A successful new paediatric priority review voucher for adolescent obesity would be worth approximately $80–120M at 2026 PRV market prices — factor into NPV modelling as a separate cash-flow item.
- **Key payer dynamics:** US Medicaid covers semaglutide for obesity in adults in approximately 40 states as of 2025, but paediatric Medicaid coverage for GLP-1 obesity treatment remains heterogeneous. A new entrant with a cost-effectiveness package demonstrating reduced future hospitalisation and T2D onset from adolescent treatment will be positioned to argue for Medicaid paediatric obesity benefit coverage expansion.

## TPP summary — the brief

A new asset seeking differentiation in the 12–17 year pediatric obesity segment post-STEP TEENS cannot win on TBWL magnitude alone — semaglutide 2.4 mg at −16.1% is already an approved reference and tirzepatide SURMOUNT-ADOL (Phase 3 results pending) is expected to reset the efficacy bar to ~−18–20% TBWL. The commercial and regulatory opportunity lies in addressing the three gaps semaglutide has created but not filled: (1) **long-term safety** — no 3-year pubertal/bone/growth-velocity dataset exists for any approved adolescent obesity medicine; a sponsor who builds this into their pivotal OLE structure generates the only multi-year safety dossier in the class, which both regulators and paediatrician prescribers are actively requesting; (2) **weight regain prevention** — ~70–80% regain within one year of stopping semaglutide is a clinical outcome unacceptable to families who see it as treatment failure, and no maintenance protocol exists for adolescents; an asset with a pre-specified step-down maintenance sub-study generating ≤40% regain data holds a label claim no competitor can match; (3) **adherence** — for injection-averse adolescents, monthly SC or oral delivery with ≥−14% TBWL outperforms a marginally better weekly injectable on real-world prescription and persistence metrics. The tirzepatide adolescent threat is real but not definitive: SURMOUNT-ADOL will likely show better TBWL but will also file without the long-term safety and maintenance data that the paediatric prescriber community most urgently needs. The asset that pairs competitive TBWL (≥−16%, non-inferior to sema; ideally ≥−18%) with a 3-year safety OLE and a maintenance sub-study enters the market with a differentiated regulatory dossier that neither semaglutide nor tirzepatide will be able to immediately replicate.

## What makes this TPP change next cycle

1. **SURMOUNT-ADOL pivotal readout (Eli Lilly, Phase 3 tirzepatide adolescents, expected 2026):** If TBWL ≥−18% at w68 with a tolerable safety profile, tirzepatide becomes the new paediatric SoC reference comparator, the efficacy bar table primary endpoint row moves to −18–20%, and any sponsor with a pending paediatric programme must re-run their NI margin calculations. Updates: Efficacy bar (primary endpoint), competitive set (tirzepatide moves to near-approved in adolescents), and TPP summary.
2. **ACHIEVE-Adol pivotal readout (Eli Lilly, orforglipron oral GLP-1, adolescents, expected 2026–2028):** If TBWL ≥−14% TBWL orally with ≤30% nausea, the oral route becomes available in adolescents and the injectable adherence differentiation argument partially erodes. Updates: Differentiation axis 3 (dosing frequency / oral delivery), competitive set (orforglipron moves to near-approved), and safety bar (GI tolerability benchmark).
3. **STEP TEENS 3-year follow-up / additional OLE publication (Novo Nordisk):** Any Novo Nordisk publication of Tanner staging, DXA, or height velocity data from STEP TEENS long-term follow-up would partially fill the long-term safety data gap and narrow the differentiation window for that axis. Updates: Unmet need section, safety bar (resets benchmarks for pubertal and bone endpoints), and differentiation axis 1 (long-term safety).
4. **FDA Paediatric Guidance update on BMI SDS as primary endpoint (anticipated 2026–2027):** FDA has indicated that %TBWL and BMI SDS are both acceptable primary endpoints in paediatric obesity trials, but formal guidance is pending. If FDA formalises BMI SDS as a co-primary or required endpoint, efficacy bar table and trial design section require updating to specify SDS-based minimum thresholds. Updates: Efficacy bar (BMI SDS row), regulatory pathway section.
5. **UK NICE and German IQWiG paediatric obesity technology appraisals:** If either body issues a positive TA for semaglutide in adolescents (expected 2026–2027), EU reimbursement landscape improves materially and the commercial upside of the EU5 market estimate increases. Updates: Market size (EU), payer dynamics.
