# TPP — NSCLC — Adjuvant EGFR-Mutated Resected NSCLC Post-ADAURA (2026-06-05)

**Segment:** Resected Stage IB–IIIA EGFR-mutated (exon19del/L858R) NSCLC, post-curative resection, adjuvant or post-adjuvant setting. Two distinct sub-segments addressed: (A) patients completing 3 years of adjuvant osimertinib who have residual recurrence risk and no established post-completion maintenance; (B) patients who relapse after or during adjuvant osimertinib — the post-osimertinib resistance trajectory — for whom no standard 2L is approved in the early-disease-to-metastatic transition zone.

---

## Patient segment

- **Inclusion criteria (sub-segment A — post-adjuvant completion):** Histologically confirmed NSCLC with EGFR sensitising mutation (exon19del or L858R); complete surgical resection (R0) Stage IB (tumour ≥4 cm), II, or IIIA; completed standard 3-year adjuvant osimertinib course per ADAURA protocol; no radiographic recurrence at treatment completion. ECOG PS 0–1. Biomarker testing at diagnosis required; no co-occurring T790M at initiation (standard adjuvant population). ctDNA measurability desirable but not required for segment definition.
- **Inclusion criteria (sub-segment B — post-osimertinib relapse):** Same surgical history plus radiographic recurrence during or within 12 months of completing adjuvant osimertinib. Estimated ~25–35% of ADAURA-enrolled patients will recur within 5 years of completing adjuvant treatment based on updated ADAURA 5-year DFS data (68% DFS at 5 years in osimertinib arm vs 26% placebo arm; ~32% of osimertinib-arm patients still recurred or died by 5 years). Metastatic or locally advanced at recurrence in the majority.
- **Estimated annual incidence in segment (US/EU/Global):** NSCLC incidence ~234,580 (US), ~400,000 (EU5), ~2.1 million (global). EGFR-mutant fraction: ~15–20% Western populations, ~50% East Asian. Resectable early-stage (IB–IIIA) fraction of all NSCLC: ~25–30%. Applying 15% EGFR prevalence to resectable US incidence: ~8,500–10,500 new adjuvant EGFR-mutant NSCLC patients per year in the US. Post-ADAURA completion pool (3-year treatment course): a rolling cohort of ~25,000–30,000 US patients will have completed adjuvant osimertinib by 2028 given ADAURA approval date (2020), growing at ~8,500/year thereafter. Post-osimertinib relapse sub-segment (sub-segment B): ~2,500–3,500 US patients per year.
- **Current standard of care (this exact segment):**
 - Sub-segment A (post-completion): None. There is no approved agent or established monitoring protocol for the post-adjuvant completion period. Surveillance CT every 6 months per NCCN is the standard, but no pharmacological intervention exists to extend DFS benefit beyond the 3-year osimertinib course. The 3-year treatment duration in ADAURA was protocol-defined, not biologically optimised — whether extending treatment, adding a second agent, or switching to a different modality improves outcomes is unanswered.
 - Sub-segment B (relapse on/post-osimertinib): No approved standard for patients who relapse specifically from adjuvant osimertinib. Clinicians extrapolate from advanced NSCLC data: amivantamab+lazertinib (MARIPOSA-2) for platinum-eligible patients with post-osimertinib resistance; datopotamab deruxtecan (Datroway, accelerated approval 2025) for EGFR-mutated post-TKI+platinum; platinum-doublet chemotherapy as a bridge. None of these are validated in the distinct clinical context of relapse from adjuvant (vs metastatic) osimertinib, where the disease may carry different resistance biology.

## Unmet need — what the current SoC fails to deliver

- **No established post-completion strategy — the 3-year duration question is unanswered:** ADAURA used a protocol-defined 3-year adjuvant osimertinib course. The choice of 3 years was not based on biological endpoint (e.g. ctDNA clearance) but on pragmatic design. Approximately 32% of osimertinib-treated patients still recurred by 5 years. The question of whether extending to 4–5 years, switching to a different mechanism at year 3, or adding a second-line maintenance agent after completion improves OS or DFS is entirely unresolved. No trial currently addresses this. ETLM `unmet_needs`: absence of approved post-completion strategy is an explicit white space — not captured as a named unmet need in the NSCLC ETLM because the ETLM addresses metastatic disease primarily. This TPP treats it as segment-defining.
- **Post-osimertinib resistance trajectory — no validated sequencing after adjuvant relapse:** Post-osimertinib resistance in the advanced/metastatic setting involves diverse mechanisms: MET amplification (~20%), C797S on-target mutation (~10%), HER2 amplification, KRAS mutations, histological transformation to SCLC or squamous (~5–10%), and undetermined mechanisms (~40%). In the adjuvant relapse context, resistance mechanism characterisation is even less established — patients may relapse without detectable tissue at a small burden, making re-biopsy technically difficult. The relapse from adjuvant osimertinib is biologically distinct from the 1L metastatic progression context: lower tumour burden at relapse, potentially different resistance clonal architecture, and better PS at relapse enabling more aggressive therapy. No clinical trial is specifically powered for this sub-segment. ETLM `unmet_needs` entry: "Post-osimertinib resistance — no approved standard 2L for EGFR-mutant NSCLC after 3rd-gen TKI progression" — directly applicable, severity classified as critical.
- **Lack of ctDNA/MRD-guided treatment decisions:** ADAURA did not incorporate ctDNA monitoring as a trial-integral biomarker. Post-completion, patients who are ctDNA-positive are at high recurrence risk and might benefit from continued treatment; ctDNA-negative patients may be cured and expose unnecessarily to 3 years of toxicity at standard treatment. No approved ctDNA-guided decision algorithm exists for the adjuvant EGFR-mutant population. The PALACE trial (NCT06917573) and ADAPT Lung (NCT07120698) are exploring ctDNA-guided cemiplimab and sintilimab adjuvant strategies respectively, but these are unselected for EGFR mutation and are not yet powered to address post-osimertinib ctDNA decision thresholds. ETLM `unmet_needs` entry: "MRD / ctDNA monitoring — liquid biopsy-guided treatment adaptation not yet standard."
- **OS maturation — DFS benefit not yet OS-confirmed at full maturity:** ADAURA primary endpoint was DFS; 5-year DFS HR 0.20 (osimertinib) is striking. However, OS data from ADAURA are not yet fully mature (Wu et al. NEJM 2023; OS HR 0.49 at a pre-specified interim with ~40% data maturity; landmark OS data for 7-year follow-up expected ~2027). Until OS is confirmed with full maturity, the DFS benefit translating to OS cure is an assumption, not a demonstrated outcome. A fraction of DFS events are due to second primaries, intercurrent illness, or disease in the contralateral lung — not directly prevented by osimertinib. The OS gap is a regulatory and commercial risk for any future adjuvant asset targeting the same population: FDA may require OS, not just DFS, for standard approval given the 3-year mature DFS HR 0.20 sets an extremely high DFS bar.
- **Tolerability burden of 3-year TKI course — dose interruptions, long-term AE management:** Osimertinib Gr3+ AE rate in ADAURA was 20% (vs 10% placebo), primarily interstitial lung disease (ILD; 3% any grade), cardiomyopathy/QTc prolongation, and paronychia. Three years of continuous TKI administration creates cumulative toxicity burden, QoL reduction, and dose interruption cycles that are not present in the short-course adjuvant chemotherapy paradigm. Approximately 13% of ADAURA patients discontinued due to adverse events. This creates a clinical need for a tolerable post-completion continuation or switch strategy, and a commercial argument for better-tolerated alternatives.

## Competitive set

### Approved

- **Osimertinib (Tagrisso, AstraZeneca)** — adjuvant resected Stage IB–IIIA EGFR-mutant NSCLC, 3-year course. DFS HR 0.20 (ADAURA, NEJM 2020/2023). FDA approved 2020. The dominant standard; no competitor has entered this space. Commercial position: ~$5.4B global revenue 2025 (total osimertinib across all indications; adjuvant represents the fastest-growing segment). Patent protected through ~2030 in major markets (US patent expiry ~2030; EU ~2031). ADAURA OS maturity data (~2027) will be the next label-enhancing event.
- **Pembrolizumab (Keytruda, Merck)** — adjuvant NSCLC all-comer (PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091, FDA approved Jan 2023). DFS HR 0.76 all-comer. **Critical caveat:** EGFR-mutant subgroup in KEYNOTE-091 showed NO DFS benefit (HR 0.97, 95% CI 0.60–1.55) and numerical signal of harm — FDA label explicitly notes EGFR-mutant patients should not receive pembrolizumab adjuvant. Pembro adjuvant is therefore NOT a competitor in the EGFR-mutant adjuvant segment. This is a common confusion point in the competitive landscape — resolved here explicitly.
- **Nivolumab (Opdivo, BMS)** — adjuvant NSCLC post-resection, neoadjuvant + adjuvant (CheckMate 816 neoadj + CheckMate 77T). CheckMate 816 neoadjuvant nivo + chemo EFS HR 0.63 (NEJM 2022). **Same caveat as pembrolizumab:** CheckMate 816 subgroup analysis shows no EFS benefit in EGFR/ALK-altered patients (HR 1.40 in driver-mutant subgroup). Nivolumab adjuvant/neoadjuvant is NOT a competitor in the EGFR-mutant segment.
- **Alectinib (Alecensa, Genentech/Roche)** — adjuvant resected ALK-positive NSCLC. DFS HR 0.24 (ALINA, NEJM 2023). Analogous pattern to ADAURA/osimertinib — adjuvant TKI in a biomarker-defined population. Establishes the class precedent: biomarker-selected adjuvant TKI achieves HR ~0.20–0.24, setting the class DFS bar. Not a competitor to EGFR adjuvant assets but defines the pattern and the FDA paradigm.

### Phase 3 / pending readout (expected within 18 months)

- **Osimertinib + chemotherapy (FLAURA2 adjuvant extension, AstraZeneca)** — There is currently no Phase 3 trial of FLAURA2 regimen in the adjuvant setting; FLAURA2 is a 1L metastatic trial. However, the principle of osimertinib+chemo combination in resectable/adjuvant Stage II–IIIA is under investigation in ADAURA-extension cohort studies (protocol amendments pending). If AstraZeneca initiates an adjuvant osimertinib+pemetrexed+platinum Phase 3 analogous to FLAURA2, this would directly compete with any adjuvant combination entrant. No confirmed Phase 3 initiation date as of June 2026. **Watch: AZ ASCO 2026 and WCLC 2026 abstracts for adjuvant combination signal.**
- **Ivonescimab (AK112, Akeso) — HARMONi-A EGFR-mutant cohort** — Phase 3 comparing ivonescimab vs osimertinib-retreatment in EGFR-mutant NSCLC after osimertinib failure (ORR 50% vs 28%, JAMA 2024). This is not an adjuvant trial but addresses sub-segment B (post-osimertinib resistance) directly. ORR 50% in post-osi resistant setting would be the benchmark for any new post-osimertinib resistance agent. Caveat: HARMONi-A is Phase 2/3 and China-enrolled; COINS Act/BIOSECURE legislative risk applies (signals 82, 140). If ivonescimab establishes itself as the standard post-osimertinib resistance agent in China but cannot access the US market due to BIOSECURE, the US post-osimertinib resistance market remains an open opportunity.
- **Amivantamab + lazertinib (MARIPOSA-2, Janssen)** — Phase 3 in EGFR-mutant NSCLC post-osimertinib + platinum chemotherapy. OS HR 0.77 (descriptive), mPFS 6.3 mo amiva+laz vs 4.2 mo chemo. FDA approved May 2024 for post-osi+plat setting. Directly relevant for sub-segment B if relapse is platinum-eligible. Key limitation: MARIPOSA-2 enrolled platinum-pre-treated patients; adjuvant-relapse patients are platinum-naive at relapse — a biologically and clinically different context. SC amivantamab + lazertinib in platinum-naive post-adjuvant-relapse is not yet studied in a Phase 3.
- **Datopotamab deruxtecan (Datroway, Daiichi Sankyo/AZ) — TROPION-Lung08** — Phase 3 dato-DXd + pembro vs pembro+chemo in 1L EGFR-mutant (NCT04656652; readout ~2026-09). If positive, this would establish an ADC-IO combination as the 1L metastatic standard in EGFR-mutant after osimertinib failure, indirectly competing with any post-adjuvant relapse standard. However, TROPION-Lung08 is 1L metastatic (not post-adjuvant relapse context) and endpoint is PFS. Primary relevance: defines the 2L/post-osi metastatic landscape that adjuvant-relapse patients will enter.
- **Glumetinib + osimertinib (Haihe Biopharma, NCT06908772)** — Phase 3 in EGFR-mutant with MET amplification post-1L osimertinib. Expected readout ~2028-12. Addresses a defined resistance mechanism (~20% of post-osi failures). Not directly competitive with an adjuvant post-completion strategy, but relevant for sub-segment B with confirmed MET amplification at relapse.

### Phase 2 / earlier-stage notable

- **ctDNA-guided adjuvant strategies (PALACE trial NCT06917573; ADAPT Lung NCT07120698)** — ctDNA-positive patients enrolled in cemiplimab or sintilimab adjuvant IO strategies. Neither is EGFR-mutant specific; both are early-phase without adjuvant EGFR-mutant signal. However, if MRD-guided treatment selection in NSCLC adjuvant is validated in these trials, it would establish ctDNA positivity as a biomarker gating adjuvant treatment continuation or switch — directly relevant to the post-completion sub-segment A opportunity.
- **Sacituzumab tirumotecan (MK-2870, Merck) — Phase 2 in EGFR-mutated post-TKI** — ORR superiority vs docetaxel in EGFR-mutated post-TKI (Fang et al. BMJ 2025). Phase 3 (NCT06074588) enrolling for post-IO+chemo NSCLC; an EGFR-mutant subgroup is likely. If Phase 3 confirms TROP2 ADC benefit in EGFR-mutant post-osimertinib, sac-TMT would compete with amivantamab+lazertinib and dato-DXd for the sub-segment B relapse population. ADC AA-pathway scrutiny elevated post-Zynlonta (signal 79) is relevant — MK-2870 Phase 3 confirmatory is in an elevated scrutiny environment.
- **JMT101 + osimertinib vs osimertinib (Shanghai JMT-Bio, NCT06380348)** — Phase 3 in EGFR exon 20 insertion post-platinum; not directly in the sub-segment A/B ADAURA-relapse context but establishes that EGFR exon 20ins has its own emerging adjuvant/2L competitive field.

## Differentiation axes — where a new asset could win

### 1. Mechanism — ctDNA-guided or biologically-timed treatment switch at year 3

No current Phase 3 addresses whether (a) extending osimertinib beyond 3 years, or (b) switching to a different mechanism at year 3 (e.g. IO, ADC, EGFR-bispecific) improves DFS. A new asset that specifically designs a Year 3 Switch Trial — where patients completing adjuvant osimertinib are randomised to a new agent (the TPP asset) vs placebo/observation — would occupy entirely uncharted territory. No competitor addresses this axis. The differentiation is not in the mechanism alone but in the treatment-position: post-3-year-osimertinib-completion is white space with no Phase 3 data whatsoever.

**Gap:** No Phase 3 exists for the post-completion adjuvant EGFR-mutant population. Any sponsor initiating such a trial would have no direct competitive overlap for 5–7 years (the time required for a competitor to catch up once the trial starts).

### 2. Patient population — ctDNA-positive post-completion enrichment

An asset that uses ctDNA positivity post-completion of osimertinib to enrich for high-risk patients — rather than treating all completers regardless of MRD status — would be a more efficient trial design and a precision-medicine differentiator. The ADAURA population is heterogeneous: patients who remain ctDNA-negative at completion may already be cured; the ~20–30% who are ctDNA-positive (residual/micrometastatic disease) are the ones at highest recurrence risk. A biomarker-selected TPP that targets ctDNA-positive post-completion patients (an enriched high-risk cohort) would have: a cleaner efficacy signal (higher event rate), smaller trial size, and a companion diagnostic story that supports premium pricing.

**Gap:** No ctDNA-gated adjuvant EGFR-mutant trial exists in Phase 3. This is also the highest unmet patient-selection need (ETLM `unmet_needs` entry: "MRD / ctDNA monitoring").

### 3. Durability — treatment-free interval after a defined-duration regimen

The ADAURA precedent fixed a 3-year course — creating an expectation that adjuvant EGFR-mutant treatment has a defined duration, not indefinite maintenance. An asset designed as a 1-year consolidation course after osimertinib completion (not indefinite maintenance) would align with patient preference for treatment completion and would be commercially differentiated from a "add another 3 years of TKI" paradigm. A defined-duration consolidation (e.g. a combination of ctDNA-guided IO + TKI for 12 months at Year 3–4) with a treatment-free interval thereafter would be a novel treatment paradigm not yet explored in any Phase 3 in this population.

**Gap:** No competitor has established a defined-duration post-completion consolidation paradigm. Patients and clinicians both prefer treatment completion over indefinite maintenance — an asset with a defined endpoint has commercial and quality-of-life advantages over indefinite extension.

### 4. Route / convenience — oral agent or subcutaneous format enabling community administration

Osimertinib is oral (daily). Any post-completion continuation or switch asset that is also oral (or subcutaneous with infrequent dosing) maintains the community-oncology accessibility of adjuvant TKI treatment. An IV bispecific (amivantamab IV) or a cytotoxic regimen would be a step backward in convenience for an adjuvant population who are otherwise well (PS 0–1) and require >1 year of treatment. SC amivantamab or a new oral EGFR-targeted agent (e.g. a 4th-gen EGFR TKI designed to address the C797S+T790M tandem resistance mutation that emerges post-3rd-gen TKI) would be preferred over IV treatment in the community setting.

**Gap:** No 4th-gen EGFR oral TKI is approved; BI-4020 (Boehringer Ingelheim) is Phase 1/2, targeting C797S + T790M — if this mechanism is confirmed in post-adjuvant relapse, the class would address both sub-segment B (resistance mechanism) and potentially sub-segment A (prevention of C797S emergence in the post-completion ctDNA+ patient). Currently no competitor addresses the 4th-gen oral axis specifically.

### 5. Combinability — IO backbone restricted in EGFR-mutant context

EGFR-mutant NSCLC is a known IO-resistance population: KEYNOTE-091 subgroup shows HR 0.97 (no benefit) for adjuvant pembrolizumab; 1L metastatic EGFR-mutant IO combinations (pembro+chemo) are inferior to osimertinib. Any strategy that pairs IO with TKI in the adjuvant completion period must overcome this IO-resistance data. A combination that sidesteps the IO axis — e.g. a TROP2 ADC (dato-DXd) consolidation or an EGFR/MET bispecific (amivantamab) post-completion — would not face IO-resistance biology concerns. The combinability differentiator here is NOT adding IO; it is finding a non-IO mechanism that can extend DFS post-completion.

**Gap:** No non-IO combinability strategy is in Phase 3 for post-completion adjuvant EGFR-mutant. FLAURA2-style osimertinib+chemo in the adjuvant setting has not been tested. ADC consolidation (dato-DXd 1 year post-completion) is a credible hypothesis with no Phase 3 data.

### 6. Cost / payer — post-completion treatment cost in a cured-disease context

Adjuvant treatment is unusual from a payer perspective: patients who are potentially cured (ctDNA-negative at completion) do not require further treatment, but the oncologist cannot distinguish them from those who will relapse without a biomarker. A ctDNA-gated strategy (treat only ctDNA-positive completers) is the most payer-defensible approach — it restricts drug use to those at demonstrably elevated risk. Post-completion osimertinib continuation (extending beyond 3 years) would require AstraZeneca to demonstrate incremental OS benefit in an extension trial — without this, payers would not reimburse osimertinib Year 4–5 beyond the existing approved duration. Any new entrant with a ctDNA-gated design would have a cleaner value proposition: treat the ~20–30% of completers at high risk, not all completers. Net addressable market is smaller but the cost-effectiveness story is stronger. At ~$17,000/month (osimertinib US list price), an asset costing ≤$12,000/month for a 12-month defined course in ctDNA-positive patients (~25% of completers) would be value-competitive under ICER frameworks.

## Regulatory & commercial reality

- **Pathway:** Breakthrough Therapy Designation achievable for a post-completion strategy in EGFR-mutant adjuvant NSCLC — the indication meets BTD criteria (serious condition, preliminary evidence of substantial improvement over available therapy). There is no approved agent for the post-completion cohort and DFS HR 0.20 in on-treatment ADAURA establishes the pre-clinical framework. Accelerated approval on ctDNA clearance or DFS in ctDNA-selected patients (surrogate for OS) is a possible regulatory pathway for Phase 2/3 evidence — but the FDA's heightened scrutiny of accelerated approval in the post-Zynlonta ADC class context (signal 79) warrants caution; confirmatory OS data should be planned from Phase 3 initiation.
- **Reference comparator for FDA:** Placebo or observation after completion of adjuvant osimertinib (the current standard = no treatment). For sub-segment B (post-adjuvant relapse), the comparator would be the investigator's choice chemotherapy (platinum-based or pemetrexed single-agent) — consistent with MARIPOSA-2 design, adapted for platinum-naive patients.
- **Confirmatory trial risk (AA-pathway):** Elevated in 2026 per Zynlonta signal (79/111). Any accelerated approval in this segment would require a tight confirmatory trial design with pre-specified OS maturity trigger. Traditional approval on DFS (a validated surrogate in adjuvant oncology, precedent: ADAURA itself was approved on DFS) is the preferred pathway — FDA has historically accepted DFS for adjuvant cancer approvals where OS maturity requires >5 years of follow-up.
- **Market size (US + EU):** Post-completion adjuvant pool (sub-segment A): ~25,000–30,000 US patients by 2028, growing at ~8,500/year. At $12,000/month for a 12-month ctDNA-positive-gated course (~25% of completers = ~2,100–2,500 treated patients/year), annual US peak revenue is ~$300–380M at full penetration. If all-comers (not ctDNA-gated): ~$1–1.2B US annually at osimertinib reference pricing. EU5 adds ~50% volume. Post-osimertinib relapse sub-segment B: ~2,500–3,500 US patients/year; at $15,000/month × 9-month median treatment course, ~$400–630M US annually if an asset achieves 60% market penetration vs current fragmented standard. Combined addressable opportunity: US $700M–$1.8B depending on ctDNA gating and combined sub-segment coverage.
- **Payer dynamics:** NCCN Category 1 in the adjuvant EGFR-mutant setting requires randomised Phase 3 DFS or OS data. Any new entrant needs Phase 3 to access guideline-driven coverage. Real-world evidence from ADAURA 5-year follow-up (OS data ~2027) will be cited by payers as the benchmark — a new asset must demonstrate additive DFS/OS benefit vs osimertinib completion, not vs placebo alone. PBM and insurer coverage of adjuvant oral TKIs is well-established (osimertinib Part D coverage, ~95% formulary coverage at Tier 2–3); a new asset in the same oral category would enter a known coverage framework.
- **Geopolitical risk:** Ivonescimab (HARMONi-A cohort addressing sub-segment B) faces COINS Act/BIOSECURE risk (signals 82, 138, 140) for US commercialisation. Glumetinib (Haihe) and JMT101 (Shanghai JMT-Bio) are also China-origin assets in the post-osi resistance setting — both face the same geopolitical headwinds. A Western-origin asset in the post-adjuvant relapse setting (sub-segment B) would be COINS Act-insulated and would benefit from the US payer preference for supply-chain-secure oncology treatments.

## TPP summary — the brief

Adjuvant EGFR-mutant NSCLC post-ADAURA represents two distinct and overlapping opportunities: the post-completion cohort (sub-segment A, ~8,500 new US patients/year after completing 3-year osimertinib, with no approved follow-on agent and ~32% 5-year recurrence risk despite best SoC) and the post-osimertinib relapse cohort (sub-segment B, ~2,500–3,500 US patients/year, no validated standard adapted to the adjuvant-relapse context). A new asset must target one of these sub-segments with a specific trial design — the post-completion Year 3 Switch Trial is entirely uncontested in Phase 3, while the relapse sub-segment has partial coverage from amivantamab+lazertinib (MARIPOSA-2) but with a critical design gap: MARIPOSA-2 enrolled platinum-pre-treated metastatic patients, not platinum-naive adjuvant-relapse patients who are a biologically distinct and clinically better-prognosis population. The highest-value differentiation axis is ctDNA-gated sub-segment A trial design — enrich for the ~20–30% of completers who are ctDNA-positive (the true high-risk cohort at Year 3), use a defined-duration 12-month consolidation rather than indefinite extension, and choose a non-IO mechanism (IO is ineffective in EGFR-mutant adjuvant per KEYNOTE-091 subgroup) such as a TROP2 ADC, EGFR-bispecific, or 4th-gen EGFR TKI. An asset meeting this profile — ctDNA-selected, defined-duration, non-IO, oral or SC formulation — would be the first agent with a Phase 3 design in the post-adjuvant-completion EGFR-mutant population, would face no head-to-head competitor for 5–7 years, and would address the most commercially underexplored segment in the osimertinib franchise lifecycle.

## What makes this TPP change next cycle

1. **ADAURA 7-year OS maturity data (~2027, AstraZeneca)** — Full OS confirmation from ADAURA with ~80% data maturity will define whether adjuvant osimertinib translates to an OS cure in Stage IB–IIIA EGFR-mutant NSCLC. If OS HR remains near the DFS HR 0.20 (unlikely at full maturity but possible), the post-completion unmet need is reduced. If OS HR attenuates toward 0.50–0.70 (more likely with longer follow-up and emerging resistance events), the post-completion recurrence risk is confirmed and the sub-segment A opportunity hardens. Updates: Patient segment (OS maturity redefines recurrence risk denominator); Efficacy bar (OS benchmark becomes the primary target).
2. **COINS Act biotech expansion vote (~Q3 2026, US Congress)** — If COINS Act expands to cover Akeso/Haihe/JMT-Bio EGFR-resistance assets, the sub-segment B competitive landscape loses its China-origin entries and the open market for Western EGFR resistance assets expands materially. Updates: Competitive set (ivonescimab HARMONi-A, glumetinib, JMT101 risk-flagged as US-ineligible); Differentiation axis 6 (geopolitical advantage for Western sponsors confirmed).
3. **TROPION-Lung08 readout (~2026-09, Daiichi Sankyo/AZ)** — If dato-DXd + pembrolizumab vs pembro+chemo in 1L EGFR-mutant post-osi shows PFS benefit, this indirectly shapes the sub-segment B relapse landscape by establishing a new 2L SoC for post-adjuvant-relapse patients who enter the metastatic setting. Importantly, this data would also validate TROP2 ADC mechanism in EGFR-mutant — supporting the ADC consolidation hypothesis for sub-segment A. Updates: Competitive set (Dato-DXd escalates as relapse standard competitor); Differentiation axis 5 (TROP2 ADC combinability in EGFR-mutant validated or refuted).
4. **ctDNA adjuvant trial interim readouts (PALACE NCT06917573; ADAPT Lung NCT07120698, ~2027–2028)** — If a ctDNA-guided adjuvant IO strategy shows DFS signal (even in unselected or non-EGFR population), it validates the ctDNA-gated trial design paradigm and creates regulatory precedent for ctDNA-positive enrichment as an approved biomarker gating strategy. This would strengthen the ctDNA differentiation axis for any sub-segment A asset and may reduce the regulatory novelty risk of using ctDNA as a gating biomarker. Updates: Differentiation axis 2 (ctDNA gating becomes validated vs speculative); Regulatory section (FDA acceptance of ctDNA as surrogate endpoint in adjuvant NSCLC confirmed or not).
