# TPP — NSCLC — 1L All-Comer Bispecific vs PD-1+Chemotherapy (2026-06-05)

**Segment:** 1L metastatic NSCLC, all-comer (no actionable driver, unselected by PD-L1), bispecific antibody strategy vs current PD-1+chemotherapy backbone. Reference comparator: pembrolizumab+pemetrexed+platinum (KEYNOTE-189/407 class) and the emerging ivonescimab HARMONi-3 data as the bispecific class anchor.

---

## Patient segment

- **Inclusion criteria:** Histologically confirmed metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma or squamous), ECOG PS 0–1, no actionable genomic alteration (EGFR/ALK/ROS1/RET/MET exon14/BRAF V600E/NTRK/HER2 exon20ins negative), systemic treatment naive. PD-L1 unselected (all-comers, including PD-L1 <1%). Biomarker testing required to exclude drivers; STK11/KEAP1 co-mutation status collected but not used for exclusion in the bispecific hypothesis.
- **Estimated annual incidence in segment (US/EU/Global):** ~60,000 US / ~80,000 EU / ~900,000 global. Derived: US NSCLC incidence ~234,580; subtract ~20% actionable drivers (EGFR+ALK+ROS1+RET+MET+BRAF+NTRK+HER2) leaves ~80% driver-negative. Of those, ~30–35% are 1L metastatic candidates (vs early/locally advanced), adjusted for PS2+ and frailty exclusions.
- **Current standard of care (this exact segment):** Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + carboplatin/cisplatin (non-squamous, KEYNOTE-189) or pembrolizumab + carboplatin + paclitaxel/nab-paclitaxel (squamous, KEYNOTE-407). mOS ~23–26 months (non-squamous updated 5yr cohort, JCO 2023). 5-year OS rate ~19%. For PD-L1 ≥50% patients, pembrolizumab monotherapy (KEYNOTE-024 mOS ~30 months) remains an option — but the bispecific TPP targets the all-comer unselected population where chemo-IO doublets are standard.

---

## Unmet need — what the current SoC fails to deliver

- **Efficacy plateau in driver-negative all-comer population:** Pembro+chemo ORR ~45–58%, mPFS 6–9 months, 5-year OS ~19%. The majority of patients (60–70%) progress within 12 months. The immunosuppressive microenvironment (VEGF, TGF-β, adenosine) limits durable IO benefit even with chemotherapy priming. ETLM `unmet_needs`: STK11/KEAP1 co-mutation (~20% non-squamous) is primary IO resistance — current pembro+chemo is largely ineffective in this subgroup (mOS ~6–8 months in STK11-mutant).
- **No improvement in squamous SoC beyond KEYNOTE-407:** Squamous patients (~25–30% of NSCLC) have fewer targetable alterations. Pembro+carboplatin+paclitaxel remains standard but 5-yr OS 18.4% — numerically worse than non-squamous. TROP2 ADC access in squamous is limited. ETLM `unmet_needs` entry: "Squamous NSCLC — fewer targetable drivers; historically poor outcomes."
- **VEGF-driven immunosuppression as targetable co-mechanism:** VEGF suppresses T-cell trafficking and enhances regulatory T-cell infiltration. A PD-1+VEGF bispecific (ivonescimab class) hypothetically breaks both axes simultaneously with one molecule vs separate bevacizumab combos, which carry higher cardiovascular/haemorrhagic risk and IV schedule burden.
- **PD-L1-low/negative patients underserved by IO monotherapy:** PD-L1 <50% patients (~70% of all-comers) require chemo backbone. A bispecific providing dual IO+anti-angiogenic activity in PD-L1-unselected patients could reduce chemotherapy dependency.
- **No OS data for any bispecific in 1L all-comer NSCLC until HARMONi-3 ASCO 2026:** ivonescimab HARMONi-3 OS HR 0.66 (ASCO 2026, KB event_id 7) is the first positive OS readout for any PD-1×VEGF bispecific vs PD-1 inhibitor in 1L NSCLC. This data point both defines the ceiling and exposes the bar a Western-enrolled competitor would need to clear.

## Competitive set

### Approved

- **Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + carboplatin/cisplatin** (Merck), 1L non-squamous all-comer, mOS ~23–26 mo. 5-yr OS 19.4% (KEYNOTE-189 JCO 2023). Dominant standard; every 1L all-comer bispecific must show OS benefit vs this backbone.
- **Pembrolizumab + carboplatin + paclitaxel/nab-paclitaxel** (Merck), 1L squamous, 5-yr OS 18.4% (KEYNOTE-407 JCO 2023). Standard for squamous histology.
- **Pembrolizumab monotherapy** (Merck), 1L PD-L1 ≥50%, mOS ~30 mo (KEYNOTE-024). Not all-comer but competitive in the PD-L1-high subset who might be candidates for a chemo-free bispecific.
- **Cemiplimab + platinum chemotherapy** (Regeneron/Sanofi), 1L all-comer, mOS 22.1 months (EMPOWER-Lung 3). An alternative PD-1+chemo option; not differentiated vs pembrolizumab+chemo at the class level.
- **Nivolumab + ipilimumab** (BMS), 1L all-comer (TMB-high enrichment), mOS 17.1 mo in unselected (CheckMate 227). Less relevant for unselected all-comer; dual checkpoint carries higher irAE burden (~33% Gr3+ irAE).

### Phase 3 / pending readout (expected within 18 months)

- **Ivonescimab (AK112; PD-1×VEGF bispecific)**, Akeso / Summit Therapeutics. HARMONi-3 OS primary endpoint met at ASCO 2026 (OS HR 0.66, 34% reduction in death risk vs PD-1 inhibitor alone in 1L all-comer NSCLC). **This is the current bispecific class benchmark.** Publication in NEJM/Lancet Oncol expected within weeks (post-ASCO buzz window closes ~Jun 12, 2026 per calendar_context). Critical caveats: (1) China-enrolled population — Western generalisability unconfirmed; (2) BIOSECURE/COINS Act legislative overhang could block US NDA pathway for Akeso-origin asset (three-signal convergence documented in cycle4 analyst review Edit 0b); (3) HARMONi-3 comparator was PD-1 inhibitor, not PD-1+chemo — head-to-head vs full chemo-IO standard not yet demonstrated. Expected US submission timeline unclear. Potential to reshape the 1L all-comer standard in non-COINS Act scenarios.
- **Domvanalimab + zimberelimab + chemotherapy** (Arcus / Gilead), TIGIT+PD-1+chemo in 1L NSCLC (NCT04736173 Ph2 readout ~2027-05). Phase 2 only; TIGIT space narrowing after tiragolumab failures. Moderate probability of displacement unless Phase 3 confirmatory shows additive TIGIT benefit >PD-1+chemo alone.
- **Pembrolizumab + sacituzumab tirumotecan (MK-2870) maintenance** (Merck), 1L metastatic maintenance strategy (NCT06422143, readout ~2029-01). Phase 3 enrolling; too early for near-term displacement risk but signals Merck's intent to defend 1L franchise with IO+ADC combos.
- **Cadonilimab (AK104; PD-1×CTLA-4 bispecific)** (Akeso), unresectable Stage III post-chemoRT consolidation (NCT06617416, readout ~2026-10, n=560). Not a direct 1L metastatic all-comer competitor but establishes Akeso's bispecific class breadth in NSCLC. COINS Act headwinds affect this program equally.

### Phase 2 / earlier-stage notable

- **Cadonilimab (PD-1×CTLA-4)** — Akeso's second bispecific in NSCLC; if Stage III consolidation Phase 3 reads positive, Akeso would hold two NSCLC bispecifics simultaneously. The dual-CTLA-4 mechanism adds T-cell priming vs ivonescimab's anti-angiogenic mechanism; different and potentially complementary patient populations.
- **TNG462 + RMC-9805** (NCT06922591) — experimental combination for NFE2L2/KEAP1-mutant NSCLC. Earlier-stage but targets the STK11/KEAP1-mutant primary IO-resistance segment that bispecifics currently cannot rescue.
- **Domvanalimab (TIGIT) combinations** — multiple TIGIT+PD-1 combinations have failed or underperformed. Domvanalimab Phase 2 is ongoing but class headwinds are significant after tiragolumab SKYSCRAPER-01 failure.

## Differentiation axes — where a new asset could win

### 1. Mechanism — dual PD-1+VEGF co-blockade vs sequential blockade

Ivonescimab already occupies this axis with Phase 3 OS data. A competitor bispecific on the same PD-1×VEGF target must demonstrate: (a) superior efficacy (OS HR <0.62 vs chemo-IO comparator), or (b) superior safety (particularly squamous safety without haemoptysis risk), or (c) a different second target (e.g., PD-1×TIM-3, PD-1×LAG-3, PD-1×CTLA-4 at lower toxicity). No PD-1×VEGF competitor besides ivonescimab has Phase 3 data in 1L all-comer NSCLC. MerckKGaA has a PD-1×VEGF bispecific (M7824/bintrafusp alfa; but TGF-β is the second target, not VEGF — that program had mixed results). Summit Therapeutics is ivonescimab's US licensing partner — no independent Western bispecific program exists as a near-term threat.

**Gap:** The PD-1×VEGF axis is Akeso-monopolised in Phase 3 data. A Western-enrolled, non-China-origin PD-1×VEGF bispecific would address the generalisability gap and sidestep COINS Act/BIOSECURE risk. This is the highest-value differentiation axis for a new entrant.

### 2. Patient population — STK11/KEAP1-mutant enrichment

Current bispecifics, including ivonescimab (HARMONi-3), do not enrich for STK11/KEAP1-mutant subgroups despite this being the largest IO-resistance cohort (~20% non-squamous). A bispecific with mechanism plausibility for STK11/KEAP1-mutant tumours (e.g., dual VEGF+TGF-β or VEGF+adenosine targeting alongside PD-1) and a biomarker-selected Phase 3 design could carve a segment. None of the current competitive set addresses this specifically at Phase 3. **This is white space.**

**Gap:** No approved or Phase 3 bispecific demonstrates OS benefit specifically in STK11/KEAP1-mutant NSCLC.

### 3. Durability — extending DoR beyond IO+chemo standard

Pembrolizumab+chemo median DoR ~12 months; ivonescimab HARMONi-3 DoR data not yet published. If a bispecific demonstrates DoR ≥18 months, with maintained responses at 24 months (≥30% responders still responding), it establishes durable disease control as a differentiator. The VEGF component should theoretically reduce acquired IO resistance via vascular normalisation — this hypothesis needs to be tested explicitly in the Phase 3 design (landmark DoR analysis at 12 and 24 months as pre-specified secondary endpoints).

**Gap:** DoR as a pre-specified secondary with OS landmark analyses is not standard in current bispecific Phase 3 designs. A competitor that builds this into the design creates a more defensible commercial narrative.

### 4. Route / convenience — outpatient IV with reduced infusion time or subQ option

Pembrolizumab is q3w or q6w IV (rapid 30 min infusion); amivantamab is IV (first dose 1,000 mg over 5h; subsequent doses shorter) or subQ. A bispecific that achieves q4w or q6w dosing with a 30–60 min IV infusion would match the pembrolizumab convenience benchmark and enable outpatient-only administration — critical for real-world adoption. The VEGF component of PD-1×VEGF bispecifics has not been dose-explored aggressively for reduced frequency dosing. Anti-VEGF antibodies (bevacizumab) are q3w but could be maintained at q6w with flat dosing if PK supports.

**Gap:** No PD-1×VEGF bispecific has demonstrated q6w or less frequent dosing acceptable to maintain VEGF suppression. This is both a pharmaceutical development opportunity and a payer access lever.

### 5. Combinability — chemo-free bispecific backbone for selected patients

The HARMONi-3 comparator was PD-1 inhibitor alone, not PD-1+chemo. If ivonescimab alone (without chemotherapy) showed OS HR 0.66 vs PD-1 monotherapy, the implication is that the PD-1×VEGF combination intrinsically enhances efficacy without chemotherapy. A new bispecific that specifically designs a chemo-free arm (bispecific alone vs pembro+chemo as comparator) in PD-L1-unselected patients would directly test whether dual IO+anti-angiogenic is sufficient to eliminate chemo. This is the hardest regulatory pathway but the highest commercial prize — a chemo-free 1L for all-comer NSCLC.

**Gap:** No Phase 3 is currently testing a PD-1×VEGF bispecific alone vs PD-1+chemo in a chemo-free design. Ivonescimab is tested vs PD-1 monotherapy (not vs PD-1+chemo). A chemo-free bispecific design is differentiated from both ivonescimab and pembro+chemo standards simultaneously.

### 6. Cost / payer — non-China-origin manufacturing, COINS Act insulation

The macro context (signals 82, 138, 140) creates a structural payer/access advantage for any Western-manufactured bispecific vs Akeso/Summit ivonescimab. US payers, particularly under potential BIOSECURE restrictions, may preferentially cover a non-China-origin bispecific even if slightly less effective, given supply chain security requirements (analogous to the CHIPS Act logic for semiconductors). Summit Therapeutics holds US rights to ivonescimab but manufacturing remains Akeso-based. A Western-origin program would be COINS Act-insulated and eligible for priority US payer contracting.

**Gap:** No Western-origin PD-1×VEGF bispecific has Phase 3 data. The first Western program to show OS HR ≤0.75 in a global trial with non-China enrollment predominance would occupy this regulatory/commercial advantage.

## Regulatory & commercial reality

- **Pathway:** Breakthrough Therapy Designation likely achievable for a PD-1×VEGF bispecific in 1L NSCLC all-comer based on HARMONi-3 precedent (ivonescimab established proof-of-concept in class). FDA requires Phase 3 demonstration; BT designation would accelerate rolling review but not remove Phase 3 requirement given existing approved standards. Fast-Track also available given unmet need in PD-L1-low/STK11-mutant subgroups.
- **Reference comparator for FDA:** Pembrolizumab + platinum-based chemotherapy (KEYNOTE-189 regimen for non-squamous; KEYNOTE-407 regimen for squamous) is the natural pivotal comparator for OS-primary 1L all-comer NSCLC. The FDA would not accept PD-1 inhibitor alone as the comparator (ivonescimab HARMONi-3 design) for a Western Phase 3 if the standard-of-care is pembro+chemo — this is a design risk for any ivonescimab US registration strategy and a design advantage for a competitor that uses pembro+chemo as the control arm.
- **Confirmatory trial risk (AA-pathway):** Not directly applicable to a bispecific antibody approved on OS primary endpoint (OS-based approvals are traditional approval, not accelerated approval). However, if any bispecific seeks accelerated approval on PFS or ORR, the current ADC class confirmatory failure risk (Zynlonta signal, macro _macro.json ADC safety theme) has elevated FDA scrutiny of confirmatory trial designs broadly. A bispecific seeking traditional approval on OS avoids AA-pathway risk entirely.
- **Market size (US + EU):** 1L all-comer metastatic NSCLC (driver-negative): ~60,000 US + ~80,000 EU = ~140,000 patients annually in Western markets. At $12,000–$15,000/month net WAC (pembrolizumab reference price ~$10,500 list/month; bispecifics command premium), the US peak revenue opportunity for a leading bispecific is $3–5B annually in this segment alone. Currently, pembrolizumab (Keytruda) captures ~$8B/yr globally across all NSCLC indications; a bispecific that partially displaces pembro+chemo in 1L all-comer would capture the highest-value unselected patient segment.
- **Payer dynamics:** PBM and payer coverage of bispecifics in 1L NSCLC will follow NCCN Category 1 designation, which requires Phase 3 OS data. No commercial bispecific currently holds NCCN Category 1 in 1L all-comer NSCLC. Ivonescimab would need to establish NCCN guideline entry after US approval, potentially 12–18 months post-approval. A payer value-based contract framing OS benefit vs chemo-IO (number-needed-to-treat analysis vs mOS improvement in months) will be critical. Real-world quality-of-life data (chemotherapy avoidance if chemo-free design succeeds) adds non-OS value evidence.
- **Geopolitical risk (China-origin assets):** Directly material. Ivonescimab (Akeso) faces a three-signal legislative convergence: COINS Act expansion to biotech (House bill, signal 82), BIOSECURE expansion (signal 140), House Appropriations IND acceleration citing China competition (signal 138). If COINS Act passes, Akeso/Summit US commercialisation pathway may require manufacturing restructuring. A Western-enrolled, Western-manufactured competitor bispecific is insulated from this risk — this is a structural commercial advantage that may override modest efficacy differences at payer/formulary level.

## TPP summary — the brief

A new bispecific targeting 1L all-comer NSCLC must beat pembrolizumab+chemotherapy on OS (HR ≤0.75, mOS ≥30 months) in a globally enrolled Phase 3, not merely beat PD-1 monotherapy as HARMONi-3 did; the FDA will require the chemo-IO standard as the comparator arm. The HARMONi-3 OS HR of 0.66 sets the bispecific class proof-of-concept but is undermined by China-population generalisability questions and an emerging three-signal COINS Act/BIOSECURE legislative risk that could block Akeso/Summit's US path entirely — creating a structural market opening for any Western-manufactured PD-1×VEGF or dual-checkpoint bispecific that can replicate or improve on ivonescimab's HR in a Western-enrolled trial. The highest-differentiation opportunity is a chemo-free bispecific design (bispecific alone vs pembro+chemo) in PD-L1-unselected patients, or a bispecific specifically designed to address the STK11/KEAP1-mutant primary IO-resistance cohort (~20% non-squamous), neither of which is addressed in any current Phase 3. An asset that demonstrates OS HR ≤0.70 vs pembro+chemo in a ≥30% STK11/KEAP1-mutant pre-stratified Phase 3 would represent a genuine step-change — that profile remains unoccupied in the competitive landscape as of June 2026.
