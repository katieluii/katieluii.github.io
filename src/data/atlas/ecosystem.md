### Target/modality crowding

- Alzheimer's disease picked up three new pre-efficacy entrants at once this week — a fourth compound in one major sponsor's own anti-amyloid franchise, tested head-to-head against several of its other amyloid mechanisms under a single master trial design; a second-generation antibody-like compound from a second major sponsor moving into an early symptomatic population; and a first-time oral small-molecule modulator of amyloid oligomers from a smaller specialist sponsor. Within the tracked universe this is the largest one-day jump in early-stage amyloid programs logged this year; an external anchor (a fresh registry pull across every amyloid-directed mechanism) would be needed before calling the space more or less crowded industry-wide.
- Atopic dermatitis added a genuinely first-in-class dual-mechanism bispecific this week, pairing a skin-barrier-repair mechanism with an interleukin-13-blocking mechanism — the first such combination logged in this indication — plus a second, mechanistically undisclosed China-origin entrant. A third addition reinforces a class this file has tracked broadening across skin and joint disease for over a year: an oral inhibitor of two intracellular signalling kinases (with one family member specifically spared), already tracked here only in rheumatoid arthritis, is now being tested in atopic dermatitis for the first time.
- Colorectal cancer added two China-origin combination entrants this week: an oral compound engineered to work around a resistance mechanism common to an older targeted-therapy class, paired with an existing antibody backbone; and a bispecific already on a priority regulatory track elsewhere for a rare neuroendocrine cancer, now being tested in colorectal cancer for the first time. Both layer onto a colorectal-cancer landscape already crowded with multiple targeted-combination and antibody-drug-conjugate approaches.
- Breast cancer added one new oral, mutation-selective agent in the post-endocrine-resistant hormone-receptor-positive setting this week, a class already anchored by three approved late-line options; framed as movement, not a crowding total — an external anchor is needed for any industry-wide count.
- Crohn's disease logged its first oral live bacterial therapeutic within the tracked universe this week — an engineered probiotic-style strain designed to express a therapeutic surface protein — entering early safety testing. One data point, too thin (small trial, safety-only) to call a modality trend, but a first appearance worth flagging.

### Modality trajectory

- Dual/multi-mechanism combination logic kept spreading beyond oncology this week: a bispecific pairing a skin-barrier-repair mechanism with a cytokine-blocking mechanism entered dermatology for the first time, and a checkpoint-times-costimulatory bispecific already progressing toward a first-ever approval in a rare cancer moved into a much larger, common solid tumor for the first time — a read-across expansion of already-validated combination logic into bigger addressable populations, not a new mechanism.
- A major sponsor's individualized cancer-vaccine program in colorectal cancer was discontinued this week after an independent safety monitoring board flagged more deaths in the treatment arm than the control arm during adjuvant, disease-free use — a caution specific to the adjuvant, minimal-residual-disease setting rather than a verdict on personalized cancer vaccines generally.
- Oral live bacterial therapeutics (engineered probiotic-style strains) logged their first appearance in the inflammatory-bowel space tracked here this week — one data point, not yet a trend, but a modality this file has not previously tracked in gut disease.
