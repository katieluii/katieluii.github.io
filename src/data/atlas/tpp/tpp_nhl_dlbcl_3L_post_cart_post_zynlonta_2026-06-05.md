# Target Product Profile
## Indication: 3L+ DLBCL — Post-CAR-T, Post-Zynlonta-Withdrawal
**Date:** 2026-06-05
**Segment:** 3L+ relapsed/refractory DLBCL in patients who have previously received a CD19 CAR-T (axi-cel, liso-cel, or tisa-cel) and are now being treated in a landscape where loncastuximab tesirine (Zynlonta) is effectively withdrawn following its June 2026 confirmatory trial safety failure.
**Status:** Open competitive set — bispecific antibody era primary standard
**Source data:** WS12 KB (nhl_dlbcl.json), WS9 ETLM (nhl_dlbcl.json), analyst review cycle 4 (2026-06-05)

---

## 1. Segment Definition and Epidemiology

### Patient population
- DLBCL patients who have received 2 prior lines of therapy including a CD19 CAR-T (axi-cel or liso-cel as 2L per ZUMA-7/TRANSFORM) or as 3L+ (tisa-cel JULIET setting)
- Subset relapsing after CAR-T: approximately 35-45% of patients relapse within 12 months of 2L CAR-T (ZUMA-7: 4yr event-free survival ~50%; TRANSFORM ~similar)
- This population is CD19-depleted or CD19-low (antigen escape is the dominant resistance mechanism post-CAR-T)
- Zynlonta withdrawal (June 4, 2026) removes the only CD19-targeting ADC from active clinical use in this line

### Size estimate
- US DLBCL incident cases: ~18,000/year (SEER 2026)
- ~40% relapse after 1L R-CHOP or pola-R-CHP: ~7,200 patients requiring 2L
- ~35-40% of 2L patients received CAR-T (eligible and accessed): ~2,500-2,900
- ~40% of those relapse post-CAR-T within 12-18 months: ~1,000-1,200 per year addressable US 3L+ post-CAR-T pool
- This is a small but rapidly growing segment given expanding 2L CAR-T uptake post-ZUMA-7/TRANSFORM approvals

### Key biology
- CD19 antigen loss/downregulation in 30-50% post CAR-T (Majzner et al; Fry et al)
- CD19-independent immune escape: MHC-I downregulation, immunosuppressive TME, T-cell exhaustion
- Residual disease often CD19-dim or CD19-negative — targeting CD19 again has limited rationale unless re-expression documented
- Alternative antigen targets with retained expression post-CD19 therapy: CD20 (retained in ~70-80%), CD79b (retained), CD22 (retained), ROR1 (retained)
- Double-hit/triple-hit HGBL subset has worse outcomes even post-CAR-T; MYC+ biology confers intrinsic resistance

## 2. Current Standard of Care (as of June 2026)

### What changed on June 4, 2026
Zynlonta (loncastuximab tesirine, CD19 PBD-dimer ADC) effectively withdrew from active clinical use following a Phase 3 confirmatory trial signal showing approximately 3x higher death rate in the trial arm versus control. This removes the only approved CD19-targeting ADC from the 3L+ DLBCL landscape.

**Before June 4, 2026 — 3L+ post-CAR-T options:**
| Agent | Target | Setting | ORR | mOS | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loncastuximab tesirine (Zynlonta) | CD19 | 3L+ R/R DLBCL | 48% | 9.5 mo | **WITHDRAWN (safety)** |
| Glofitamab (Columvi) | CD20xCD3 | 3L+ R/R DLBCL | 52% | 11.5 mo | Approved — fixed duration IV |
| Epcoritamab (Epkinly) | CD20xCD3 | 3L+ R/R DLBCL | 63% | 18.5 mo | Approved — SC dosing |
| Selinexor (Xpovio) | XPO1 | 3L+ R/R DLBCL | 28% | 9.1 mo | Approved — low use |
| Pola-BR (Polivy + BR) | CD79b + chemo | 3L+ R/R DLBCL | 45% | 12.4 mo | Approved — chemotherapy-based |
| Tafasitamab + lenalidomide (Monjuvi) | CD19 | 2L+ non-ASCT-eligible | 60% | 33.5 mo | Approved — not CAR-T naive |

**After June 4, 2026 — active 3L+ options for post-CAR-T DLBCL:**
- CD20xCD3 bispecifics (glofitamab, epcoritamab) are now the clearest standard: CD20 is retained post-CD19 CAR-T; bispecifics redirect T-cells independent of prior CD19 CAR-T burden
- Pola-BR remains an option where CD79b expression is confirmed (not post-CD79b-ADC exposed)
- Selinexor available but modest efficacy (ORR 28%)
- Tafasitamab/lenalidomide (L-MIND): CD19 target may have limited activity post-CAR-T antigen loss; not labeled for post-CAR-T specifically
- Odronextamab (Ordspono): EU-approved but FDA CRL (manufacturing); EU option for post-CAR-T DLBCL if CD20 retained

### Key gaps in current SoC
1. **No agent validated specifically in post-CAR-T DLBCL** — all approvals predate widespread 2L CAR-T use; post-CAR-T patients are a distinct population now being enrolled in dedicated studies
2. **No CD22 or ROR1 agent approved** despite antigen retention post-CD19 therapy
3. **CAR-T re-dosing** (second CAR-T infusion) is investigational; lymphopenia, poor fitness, limited manufacturing slots are barriers
4. **Allogeneic CAR-T** (CB-010, ALLO-501A) not yet approved; Phase 2 data at ASH 2025 emerging but not confirmatory

## 3. Competitive Landscape — Active Competitors in 3L+ Post-CAR-T DLBCL

### Approved (available now)

**Glofitamab (Columvi, Roche)**
- CD20xCD3 bispecific; fixed duration (12 cycles); IV
- ORR 52%, CR 39%, mOS 11.5 mo (NP30179 Phase 2 registrational)
- STARGLO (glofitamab + GemOx vs R-GemOx, 2L+ transplant-ineligible): OS HR 0.62 — supports combination in broader R/R setting
- Post-CAR-T specific data: limited dedicated cohort; CD20 retention makes mechanism sound; T-cell redirector activity likely preserved in post-CAR-T setting
- Key advantage: fixed duration; no ongoing infusion burden; approved earlier-line combo (STARGLO)
- Key disadvantage: step-up dosing schedule complexity (CRS risk); all IV; 11.5 mo OS in monotherapy setting is modest

**Epcoritamab (Epkinly/Tepkinly, Genmab/AbbVie)**
- CD20xCD3 bispecific; continuous subcutaneous dosing
- ORR 63%, CR 39%, mOS 18.5 mo (EPCORE NHL-1 Phase 1/2) — highest mOS among single agents in 3L+ DLBCL
- EHA 2025 long-term update: CR durability plateau at ~50% of responders at 18 months — durable CRs in a meaningful minority
- Post-CAR-T data: dedicated post-CAR-T subgroup in EPCORE NHL-1 shows activity; SC administration advantage in impaired patients
- EPCORE DLBCL-3 (epcoritamab + GemOx vs R-GemOx, 2L+ TI): OS readout pending 2026-12 — pivotal for 2L+ label expansion
- Key advantage: SC; longest mOS data of any bispecific mono; ongoing treatment (continuous dosing = option for response maintenance)
- Key disadvantage: continuous indefinite dosing; SC site reactions; T-cell-mediated CRS risk; step-up schedule

**Selinexor (Xpovio, Karyopharm)**
- XPO1 SINE compound; oral
- ORR 28%, mPFS 2.6 mo, mOS 9.1 mo (SADAL Phase 2b)
- Modest efficacy; limited uptake; mainly used in heavily pretreated patients without other options
- Post-CAR-T setting: mechanistically active (XPO1 inhibition is antigen-independent) but low response rate makes it a last-resort option
- Advantage: oral; antigen-independent
- Disadvantage: 28% ORR; poor PFS; fatigue/cytopenias common

**Polatuzumab vedotin + BR (Polivy+BR, Roche)**
- CD79b vedotin ADC + bendamustine/rituximab
- ORR 45%, mPFS 9.5 mo, mOS 12.4 mo (GO29365 Phase 1b/2)
- Chemotherapy-based; CD79b must be retained; R-naive or re-sensitized patients benefit most
- Post-CAR-T: CD79b generally retained; CD20 (rituximab component) may be compromised by prior anti-CD20 therapy (most patients received rituximab); benda/rituximab myelosuppression in lymphodepleted post-CAR-T patients is a concern
- Advantage: CD79b targeting is CD19-independent; established approval
- Disadvantage: chemotherapy toxicity in already-compromised marrow reserve; bendamustine myelosuppression post-CAR-T lymphodepletion

### In active development (3L+ post-CAR-T DLBCL-relevant)

**Odronextamab (Ordspono, Regeneron)**
- CD20xCD3 bispecific; IV
- ORR ~80% in R/R FL (ELM-1); ORR data in DLBCL pending regulatory review
- FDA CRL March 2024 (manufacturing); EU approval August 2024; FDA resubmission expected 2025-2026
- Post-CAR-T: CD20-targeting bispecific — same mechanism rationale as glofitamab/epcoritamab
- OLYMPIA-3 (odronextamab + CHOP, 1L DLBCL): currently recruiting — not directly relevant to 3L+
- Competitive significance: if FDA approves, adds a third CD20xCD3 bispecific competitor; differentiation vs glofit/epco will be modest without head-to-head data

**Zilovertamab vedotin / MK-2140 (Merck)**
- ROR1 vedotin ADC; antigen-independent of CD19/CD20
- Phase 2 (waveLINE-004): R/R DLBCL and MCL
- ROR1 is expressed on DLBCL and is NOT depleted by prior CD19 CAR-T therapy — directly relevant antigen alternative
- Readout: waveLINE-004 estimated 2026-09
- Post-CAR-T significance: high — ROR1 targeting after CD19 depletion is a rational mechanistic choice; no approved ROR1 agent yet

**ADCT-602 (ADC Therapeutics)**
- CD22 PBD-dimer ADC; Phase 2 (NCT03698552)
- CD22 is retained post-CD19 therapy; CD22 targeting is mechanistically sound in post-CAR-T DLBCL
- CAUTION: Same sponsor (ADC Therapeutics) as Zynlonta. Zynlonta's confirmatory trial safety failure raises PBD-class platform risk and sponsor credibility risk for ADCT-602. ADC Therapeutics shares fell 50%+ after Zynlonta signal. Program viability risk elevated.
- Post-CAR-T significance: CD22 targeting is mechanistically appealing, but PBD class safety concerns now apply to ADCT-602

**Allogeneic CD19 CAR-T (CB-010, Caribou; ALLO-501A, Allogene)**
- Phase 2; post-CAR-T niche unclear — CD19 antigen loss limits rationale for second CD19 CAR-T (auto or allo)
- CB-010: CRISPR-edited PD-1 KO — potentially superior anti-exhaustion profile in post-CAR-T T-cell-exhausted environment
- ALPHA-2 (CB-010): ASH 2025 report — CR rate 67% in R/R LBCL; 6-month durability emerging; first off-the-shelf product approaching autologous CAR-T benchmarks
- Key limitation for post-CAR-T use: CD19 antigen escape means re-targeting CD19 (even with allo-CAR-T) may not overcome antigen-negative relapse
- Off-the-shelf advantage: turnaround weeks not months; relevant for rapidly progressing post-CAR-T relapses
- FDA CGT prior data guidance (June 2, 2026): reduces evidence burden for allo-CAR-T submissions

**JNJ-90014496 (CD20+CD79b+CD3 trispecific, Genmab/AbbVie/JNJ)**
- Phase 2 (TRION-1); targets CD20 AND CD79b simultaneously, plus CD3 T-cell engagement
- Post-CAR-T relevance: dual antigen engagement (CD20 + CD79b) may overcome single-antigen downregulation; highly relevant to post-CAR-T setting where antigen heterogeneity is the failure mechanism
- Readout: 2027-06; still early

**In vivo cell therapy (Legend Biotech, unnamed, lymphoma)**
- Complete or near-complete responses in all early recipients (June 3, 2026 signal)
- Eliminates ex vivo manufacturing — off-the-shelf logistics
- Very early (Phase 1 or earlier); China manufacturing exposure (COINS Act/BIOSECURE risk)
- Long-term strategic signal — 5-7 year horizon before clinical impact on 3L+ DLBCL

## 4. TPP: Minimum Viable Profile for 3L+ Post-CAR-T DLBCL (Approvable)

### Efficacy bar (FDA/EMA minimum)

| Endpoint | Minimum acceptable | Target / differentiated |
|---|---|---|
| ORR | >=40% (LOTIS-2/pola+BR historical bar) | >=60% (epcoritamab current best-in-class) |
| CR rate | >=20% | >=35-40% |
| Median DOR | >=4 months | >=9 months |
| Median PFS | >=4 months | >=6-9 months |
| Median OS | >=9 months (selinexor/glofit bar) | >=18 months (epcoritamab bar) |
| Dedicated post-CAR-T cohort | Strongly preferred; FDA will expect dedicated data given 2L CAR-T label expansion | Required for competitive label language |

Notes on efficacy context:
- Zynlonta achieved ORR 48%, CR 24%, mPFS 4.9 mo, mOS 9.5 mo in unselected 3L+ DLBCL — this was approvable under accelerated approval; confirmed OS benefit was not demonstrated (hence the confirmatory trial)
- CD20xCD3 bispecifics (glofitamab 52% ORR / epcoritamab 63% ORR) have now set a higher bar with CR durability data; new entrants must show competitive or mechanistically differentiated data
- In dedicated post-CAR-T DLBCL (CD19-exposed), CD20-targeting bispecifics are likely to retain activity (CD20 usually preserved); a non-CD20/non-CD19 agent must show at least equivalent ORR

### Safety bar

| Parameter | Requirement |
|---|---|
| CRS rate >=grade 3 | <10% (bispecific bar — glofit/epco both <5% G3 CRS with step-up) |
| Neurological toxicity | <5% G3 ICANS/neurotox |
| Cytopenias (G3/4) | <40% G3/4 neutropenia — critical given post-CAR-T marrow reserve |
| Infection rate G3+ | <25% — post-CAR-T immunocompromised baseline |
| Treatment discontinuation | <15% due to AEs |

Notes on safety context:
- Post-CAR-T patients typically have residual T-cell depletion, prior lymphodepletion with fludarabine/cyclophosphamide, and cumulative cytopenias — safety bar is higher than in CAR-T-naive R/R DLBCL
- PBD-class ADCs (Zynlonta, ADCT-602): the June 2026 Zynlonta confirmatory safety signal raises systemic concern about PBD-dimer toxicity in this heavily pretreated population; new PBD programs should anticipate heightened safety scrutiny
- Outpatient-compatible administration is now a standard expectation — hospitalization requirement for CRS management is a commercial liability

### Label and regulatory considerations

| Item | Minimum | Preferred |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory pathway | Accelerated approval on ORR/CR | Preferred: traditional approval on OS benefit (given Zynlonta AA failure) |
| Confirmatory trial | Required if AA pathway — must be pre-specified and powered for OS | Confirmatory trial designed at time of first submission |
| Label population | 3L+ R/R DLBCL | Explicit post-CAR-T language preferred |
| Accelerated approval risk | HIGH — FDA AA under scrutiny; post-Zynlonta failure, FDA will scrutinize AA-to-confirmatory trial pipeline more aggressively | Traditional OS endpoint de-risks from AA withdrawal |
| EMA pathway | Similar — EMA conditional marketing authorisation; requires post-authorisation confirmatory data |  |

Critical post-Zynlonta context:
The FDA accelerated approval pathway in oncology has been under increasing scrutiny (Accelerated Approval Program reform, FDORA 2022, Omnibus 2023). Zynlonta's confirmatory failure (June 2026) will accelerate FDA pressure on: (1) pre-specified confirmatory trials at time of AA grant, (2) requiring OS or EFS as confirmatory endpoints rather than ORR-only, (3) tighter timelines for confirmatory trial completion. Any new program targeting AA in 3L+ DLBCL must plan for a confirmatory OS-powered trial from the start.

## 5. Strategic White Space — What an Ideal 3L+ Post-CAR-T DLBCL Agent Looks Like

**The winning profile in 2026 is NOT another CD20xCD3 bispecific.** Epcoritamab and glofitamab have strong data and prior approval. A CD20xCD3 bispecific entrant needs meaningful differentiation (e.g., superior durability, combination with chemotherapy in transplant-ineligible, or clearly better tolerability profile). Absent that, share gains will be incremental.

**The open competitive positions are:**

### A. CD19-independent antigen targeting in CD19-escape patients
- Target: CD22, ROR1, or novel B-cell antigens retained post-CD19 CAR-T
- Mechanism: ADC (MMAE or novel payload) or bispecific (T-cell or NK-cell engaging)
- Why it wins: addresses the mechanistic gap in antigen-escape relapse; genuinely new competitive space
- Closest asset: zilovertamab vedotin (ROR1, Merck, Phase 2 waveLINE-004, readout 2026-09); ADCT-602 (CD22, PBD, sponsor credibility risk)
- Risk: ROR1 expression in DLBCL ~60-70% (not universal); enriched selection required

### B. Dual/multi-antigen T-cell engager for antigen heterogeneity
- Target: CD20 + CD79b + CD3 (trispecific); or CD19 + CD22 bispecific
- Why it wins: antigen heterogeneity post-CAR-T is the primary resistance mechanism; hitting two tumor antigens simultaneously reduces escape probability
- Closest asset: JNJ-90014496 (CD20 + CD79b + CD3 trispecific, Phase 2, readout 2027)
- Risk: safety complexity; CRS profile with dual engagement; manufacturing complexity

### C. Off-the-shelf allogeneic CAR-T with anti-exhaustion engineering
- Target: CD19 (if antigen retained) or dual CD19/CD22
- Why it wins: post-CAR-T patients progress rapidly; off-the-shelf eliminates 4-6 week autologous manufacturing wait; PD-1 KO (CB-010) or similar exhaustion engineering improves persistence in already T-cell-depleted environment
- Closest asset: CB-010 (Caribou, Phase 2, ALPHA-2) — CR 67% in R/R LBCL; FDA CBER guidance reduces evidence burden
- Caveat: CD19 antigen loss limits activity in true CD19-escape patients; dual-targeting allo-CAR-T (CD19+CD22) is the next evolution

### D. Oral/non-CRS backbone combination with bispecific
- Target: XPO1 inhibitor + CD20xCD3 bispecific; BCL2i + bispecific; CELMoD + bispecific
- Why it wins: single-agent bispecific CR durability is ~30-40%; combination with oral targeted agents (non-chemotherapy) can maintain/deepen responses without additional infusion toxicity
- Closest assets: iberdomide (CELMoD) + tafasitamab (Phase 2, CC-220-DLBCL-001); mezigdomide (CC-92480) + bispecific combos
- Risk: combination PK/PD; combinatorial CRS; overlapping immunosuppressive toxicity

## 8. Key Uncertainties and Research Questions

1. **What fraction of post-CAR-T DLBCL relapses are CD19-negative vs CD19-low vs CD19-positive?** This determines whether CD19-targeting agents (bispecifics, second CAR-T) have activity in this population — currently not well characterized in prospective data.

2. **Does CD20xCD3 bispecific activity differ in post-CAR-T vs CAR-T-naive 3L+ DLBCL?** CD20 is usually retained, but CAR-T-induced T-cell exhaustion could impair bispecific-mediated T-cell killing. Dedicated cohort data needed.

3. **Will FDA require OS confirmation vs ORR for new 3L+ DLBCL approvals post-Zynlonta?** The answer to this question determines the clinical development timeline and capital required for any new program in this segment.

4. **What is the long-term OS in the post-CAR-T DLBCL relapse population?** ZUMA-7 and TRANSFORM 4-5 year data capture most patients; post-CAR-T relapse outcomes at 2L+ are now becoming characterizable but are not yet systematically published.

5. **Can in vivo cell therapy (Legend Biotech platform) be manufactured without ex vivo steps?** If yes, competitive implications for all cell therapy programs in DLBCL at a 5-7 year horizon are profound.
